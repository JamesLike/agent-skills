## 2. Product categories

### 50/50 Skis: Backcountry & Resort
Class overview: for one setup used for both lift-access and touring. Blister still notes dedicated setups are ideal (durability, stability, safety), but advances in AT gear make it a better time than ever for one setup. Weight is the key trade-off: lighter = worse in variable inbounds conditions, better on the uphill. These skis are better in-resort than the touring section but lighter (and often less damp) than All-Mountain/Powder skis.

### Backcountry Touring Skis
Class overview: choosing is about uphill vs downhill trade-off and personal preference (see "Saving Weight in Your Backcountry Ski Setup" article). Don't fixate on ski weight — consider total weight of skis+bindings+boots+skins. If not doing long tours or racing, heavier gear offers better stability/suspension/safety. ### Ski Boots
Class overview: see an experienced boot fitter — foot shape/biomechanics and ability matter most; also be clear about how/where you ski. Class overview: BC gear can be confusing; skis/boots matter, as do sound decisions, risk assessment, trusted partners — but bindings and skins significantly affect your day since most time is spent going uphill. Range from alpine-level downhill 50/50 bindings to very light long-day bindings and in-between; "a very good time to be a backcountry skier." (Photos: Drew Kelly, Jonathan Ellsworth.)

### Helmets & Goggles
Class overview — Helmets: a properly fitting helmet can be life or death; four years ago Jonathan Ellsworth fell while bootpacking on a summer tour, went over a cliff, tomahawked through boulders, broke his neck — survived thanks to helmet, skiing again six months later after surgery (Blister Podcast #47–48). Not all helmets equal (GEAR:30 "Current State of Helmet Tech" series), but any helmet beats none; no excuses given variety. Goggles: seeing well makes skiing safer and more fun; industry improving lenses, lens-swap systems, affordability. (Photos: Luke Koppa, Drew Kelly.)

- **Biggest guide yet (8th edition)**; continued no-manufacturer-advertising stance funded by memberships; new initiatives: Blister Labs (engineering program with CU Boulder & Western Colorado University), Craft Collective + CRAFTED podcast, Open Mic series, 3rd Blister Summit (Feb 12–16, 2023), members-only content and real-time Spectrum updates.
