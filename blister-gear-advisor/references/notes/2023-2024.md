### 50/50 Skis: Backcountry & Resort [L260-~]
Class overview: for one setup used both lift-accessed and touring. Blister still says dedicated setups are ideal (durability, stability, weight, safety), but AT gear advances make a single setup more viable than ever. Weight is the key tradeoff (lighter = worse in variable inbounds snow, better uphill). These skis perform "fairly well" to "quite well" across resort conditions -- better than lighter dedicated touring skis, lighter than All-Mountain/Powder skis (though often less damp/stable). Members can get personalized recs.

### Backcountry Touring Skis [L1160-]
Class overview: choosing a touring setup = balancing uphill vs downhill performance for your objectives; personal preference (see "Saving Weight in Your Backcountry Ski Setup" article). Many good touring skis now for long days, quick laps, or tricks. Don't focus on ski weight alone -- consider skis + bindings + boots + skins. If not doing long tours or racing, heavier gear generally offers better stability, suspension, and safety features on descent.

### Women's All-Mountain Skis -- More Stable [L2335-]
Class overview: New organization this year -- more women's skis than ever, so women's all-mountain split into "More Forgiving" and "More Stable," plus a new dedicated "Women's Powder Skis" section. ### All-Mountain Skis -- More Stable [L5144-]

Good edge hold, powerful carve finishes; good mogul skis for strong skiers but punishing for less experienced; unappealing to those wanting loose/playful or very easy slow-speed initiation. ## 2b. Ski Boots [L10253-]
Section overview: see an experienced boot fitter -- foot shape/biomechanics and ability are crucial. Be clear how/where you ski. Three classes: (1) Alpine boots (riveted cuffs, no walk mode); (2) Alpine/Touring "AT" boots (alpine boots with walk/hike mode -- some tour well, some just for walking to lift/apres); (3) Touring boots (lighter, better ROM, all tech-binding compatible; less ideal for frequent resort laps due to downhill performance/durability). Manufacturers are making lighter boots in all 3 categories; tradeoffs as with skis. Terms: "progressive" flex = gets stiffer as you flex forward; "linear" flex = roughly constant support.
