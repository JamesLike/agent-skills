### Editorial letter: "Finally, a Guide You Can Trust" [pp. 2-3]
- By Jonathan Ellsworth, Editor-in-Chief. Billed as the inaugural edition, "a very different kind of buyer's guide."
- Building a better buyer's guide: mocks other guides' vague/cute copy (e.g., "scoot through crud, knit through bumps", "ready to rock from turn one") and factual errors (a ski said to get "a wider chassis" that Blister measured and found unchanged).
- How we do things: test gear every month (northern then southern hemisphere); multiple days in a wide range of conditions on every product, not a run or two; thousands of hours testing and writing; talk to manufacturing/materials experts; measure and take things apart. Takes no money from manufacturers whose products they review.
- How to use this guide: digestible shortcut/supplement to the full 1,500-3,000-word reviews on blistergearreview.com.
- Blister Spectrums: skis in each class ranked relative to one another on class-specific criteria (stability, edge hold, flotation, playfulness, etc.). Adjacent = similar; 2-3 spots apart is meaningful. Used with Class Overviews and product descriptions.
- Our photos: every skier/boarder pictured is an actual Blister reviewer on gear they were testing.
- Bottom line: promise to keep improving next year.

### Glossary [pp. 6-7]
- Snow/Conditions: Chop/Deep Chop (cut-up fresh pow; 3-5" shallow chop is easy, 12-24" trenches test stability); Crud/Mank (chopped snow that consolidates, warms, refreezes -- heavy, grabby clumps; late-season freeze-thaw yields "Coral Reef"/"Death Cookies"); Refrozen/Coral Reef (brutal, no ski handles it well); Variable Conditions (mix of firm and soft); Variable Terrain (downed trees, boulders causing undulations); Groomers: Roughed-Up ("end-of-the-day", mini-moguls/soft piles); Groomers: Smooth & Consistent (fresh corduroy); Groomers: Bulletproof (rock-hard ice; best hope off race skis is a controlled skid); Wind-Deposited/Chalk (denser pow blown in, surprise stashes); Wind-Scoured (stripped thin and firm, often grippy); Wind-Buff/Cream Cheese (dense, smooth, super rippable); Slush Bumps (ranked just behind deep pow, wind-deposited stashes, and wind-buff; around perfect corduroy).
- Skiing/Turn Styles: Jib/Jibby; Freestyle / "freestyle-oriented approach to terrain" (cat-track jumps, side hits, spinning off cliffs); Skiing Switch; Slarve/Drift/Surf (controlled power slide, originally a pow term); Directional (never skis switch, rarely slashes, forward race-inspired stance, drives skis).
- Ski design & materials: Swing Weight (how quick/sluggish, esp. at tips; heavily tapered tips = low swing weight); Taper (widest point of tip/tail drawn toward center); Titanal (aluminum alloy, "metal", wrongly called "titanium" by marketing); "Rocker" terminology -> see Rocker 101 on the website.

### Know Thyself: "On Socrates, Skiing, & Buyer's Guides" [pp. 8-9]
- Essay by Jonathan Ellsworth on the Delphic maxim gnothi seauton ("know thyself"); Socrates wise only in recognizing and owning his ignorance.
- Applied to skiing: overestimating ability leads to injury; failure to admit uncertainty about snowpack can be disastrous; be accurate in self-assessment.
- Pointed questions to readers: Are you skilled enough for that chute? Trained enough for backcountry decisions? Putting an AT binding on because you'll tour or because you like the idea? Complaining skis are too fat without knowing how to carve? Finesse vs. athleticism/power skier? Could lessons help? Mocking park skiers without skiing well outside the park?
- Practical guidance: East Coast doesn't automatically mean a sub-80 mm carver (esp. if you don't like carving groomers); think twice about the nearly center-mounted all-mountain skis raved about in this guide if you won't trick/spin them; don't talk yourself into a ski because it's cheaper than one you demoed and loved; take Avy 1/Avy 2 before buying a touring setup. Close: "Modern skis, ancient wisdom."

### All-Mountain // More Forgiving [pp. 20-27]
- pp. 20-21: section opener photo (reviewer Jonathan Ellsworth, photo Will Brown).
- Class overview [p. 22]: Directional skis that are relatively light, quick and forgiving for their sizes/widths. Unlike most skis in the previous class (and the wider All-Mountain Chargers that follow), most have no metal. Lighter, softer, easier to ski than stiffer/heavier skis of similar width, but less stable in rough, variable conditions. Generally good edge hold and carving; more pronounced tip rocker means good float for their widths. Suit beginners/intermediates wanting a dependable everyday ski, or as wider-yet-forgiving options for skiing aggressively in soft/chopped snow; also advanced/expert skiers who prefer a light, nimble ski without much tail rocker.

### All-Mountain Chargers [pp. 28-35]
- pp. 28-29: section opener photo (reviewer Jason Hutchins, photo Tom Bear). p. 30 photo: reviewer Garrett Altmann, photo Ryan Heffernan.
- Class overview [p. 30]: For skiers whose approach is big, fast turns regardless of conditions. Generally quite stiff, respond best to a forward, aggressive stance, not designed to be super forgiving. Built to stay stable at speed in chopped-up, variable snow and do well in firm conditions for their width. More demanding in moguls and trees than (a) narrower skis of similar stiffness (p. 14) and (b) similarly wide directional skis with softer flex and lighter swing weights (p. 22). "Built to rage": big top ends, hold up at speed in crap conditions.
- **Armada Invictus 108Ti** -- 138-108-128 mm (188) | 26 m (188) | 2,287 & 2,290 g (188) | tip rocker - camber - tail rocker (slight) | 168, 178, 188 cm | poplar-ash wood core, Titanal metal (2-layer), & carbon/Kevlar stringers. Released last season as a significant departure from Armada's light, playful skis: very stout, big-mountain feel strong experts will enjoy; soft and deep snow performance a notch above most others in the class. For a charger as at home in fresh pow as in mixed conditions.
- **Blizzard Cochise** -- 136-108-122 mm (185) | 28 m (185) | 2,376 & 2,393 g (185) | tip rocker (conservative) - camber - tail rocker (slight) | 171, 178, 185, 192 cm | poplar-beech wood core, Titanal metal (2-layer) & fiberglass laminate. 3rd iteration; 15/16 still a very capable charger. On hard snow it feels more locked-in on edge than in past years -- great for groomer performance, less so if you liked the looser feel. Many will like its metal-laminate feel and maneuverability; others may prefer the Line Supernatural 108's lighter, more energetic (yet remarkably stable) feel.

Lets you ski hard but also dial it back more than any other ski here. Pow, soft chop, crud, moguls, corduroy, end-of-day groomers -- very impressive range.
- **Moment Belafonte** -- 133-106-122 mm (186) | 25.5 m (186) | 2,227 & 2,249 g (186) | tip rocker (conservative) - camber w/ "Micro-Camber" sections - flat tail (half twin) | 168, 178, 186, 194 cm | aspen-ash wood core, fiberglass laminate & carbon fiber stringers. Pretty "traditional" charger: tip rocker, positive camber, flat (slightly twinned) tail. Near the top of the class for edge grip on hard snow/groomers; quite stable in variable despite no metal, though not as stable as the Wailer 105 or Invictus 108Ti -- but less demanding than either, more comparable to the Cochise. Class overview (p. 102): Built for up and down. ### Ski Boots -- Overview [p. 114]

### Alpine Boots [pp. 115-116]
- **Fischer RC4 130 Vacuum** -- flex 130 | last 98-103 mm (heat moldable). Three reviewers 50+ days each; Vacuum Fit gives the best, most precise fit they've had. ### Climbing Skins [p. 124]
- **Black Diamond GlideLite Mohair Pure STS** -- MSRP $195. Favorite 100% mohair skin: a bit less traction and durability than nylon/mix but glides more smoothly/efficiently than any skin tested. Go-to for long approaches and multi-day trips; experienced tourers with good technique can use them almost anywhere.
- **Black Diamond Ascension Nylon STS** -- MSRP $160. Among the most durable skins used; glide not as good as mohair/mix; solid, dependable, all-around, easy to recommend.
- **Black Diamond GlideLite Mohair Mix STS** -- MSRP $180. Among the most versatile: mohair glide with nylon traction; glue resists glopping well. Only fault: clips don't always attach securely on very wide tip shapes. Great on day trips and multi-day traverses.

## Outerwear 101 [pp. 134-137] (non-product article)
(Photo: Julia Van Raalte / Ryan Heffernan; p. 136 photo Paul Forward / Cy Whitling.)
- Intro: full history on the Blister website (Outerwear 101 & 201); here the essentials on waterproof/breathable fabric types, how they work/perform, and buying questions.
- **Gore-Tex & how it works:** Modern outerwear started with Gore-Tex (Bob Gore, 1976, first truly waterproof/breathable jackets). Standard Gore-Tex membrane = two thin layers: inner polyurethane (PU) layer plus outer, thicker ePTFE (porous, web-like; vapor passes, water doesn't), sandwiched between face fabric and lining. Vapor must adsorb into and diffuse (solid-state) through the PU, which limits breathability but protects the ePTFE.
- **PU laminates:** Cheaper waterproof/breathable fabrics without ePTFE, just a thicker PU layer; waterproof, breathe far less than Gore-Tex. Most proprietary house fabrics (e.g., Patagonia H2No, Flylow Intuitive) are PU laminates. In colder climates or low exertion, extra cash for Gore-Tex breathability may not be worth it.
- **"Air permeable" membranes:** Biggest development since Gore-Tex (Polartec NeoShell, eVent, Mountain Hardwear Dry.Q Elite). No non-porous PU layer, so noticeably more breathable than Gore-Tex and much more than PU laminates. Still waterproof but less so -- typically ~10,000 mm hydrostatic head, sufficient for most.
- **Fabric overview:** Gore-Tex (very durable, extremely waterproof, for harshest/wettest climates; can feel heavy/stiff, less breathable than Pro). Gore-Tex Pro (double ePTFE, no PU layer, more breathable; inner ePTFE sacrificial vs. fouling; lighter/suppler but crinkly vs. C-Knit/Active; best-in-class protection, breathes well). Gore-Tex Active (most breathable Gore fabric, only allowed on minimalist pieces, lighter but less durable). Gore-Tex with C-Knit backer (standard membrane, new 3-layer backer; softer hand like Active, a bit more breathable than Pro). Polartec NeoShell (leading air-perm; less waterproof than Gore but much more breathable; enough storm protection for drier climates, ideal for high output).
- **Quick buying tips:** Where do you live (dry/cold Rockies -> prioritize breathability; wet coastal -> waterproofness, sacrifice some breathability); What do you do (more hiking/touring -> more breathable); Why are you buying (year-round vs. holiday use; trim minimalist shell easier to ski in than to backpack in a loose freeride jacket -- one-shell quiver: light on features, athletic fit; vacation jacket: think style/features).

- **Volkl Aura, 170 cm** -- Stable, directional all-mountain ski experts will enjoy and advanced skiers won't find too demanding; traditional, predictable feel without fighting through short, quick turns.

### Best Of -- Touring Skis [p. 164]
- **G3 Zenoxide 105, 185 cm** -- Best touring ski they've used for consequential terrain or bad conditions; a bit heavier (~1,900 g/ski), which helps it hold up in rough snow; stiff, but zero complaints in fresh snow either.
- **Volkl V-Werks BMT 109, 186 cm** -- Best touring ski for mediocre-to-deep conditions; more composed downhill than its ~1,700 g/ski would suggest.
- **Blizzard Zero G 108, 185 cm** -- Remarkable sub-1,800 g touring ski if you care more about firm-snow than deep-snow performance.

### Best Of -- Bindings [p. 164]
- **Marker Kingpin 13** -- Best all-around AT binding they've used; lighter reviewers ski it daily inbounds; heavier reviewers increasingly confident in demanding terrain. Burlier/heavier frame bindings and lighter tech bindings exist, but Kingpin occupies a compelling up/down middle ground.

### Best Of -- Boots [p. 164]
- **Salomon MTN Lab** -- Best AT boot AND their best product of the year; no boot tested is so light/easy to tour in yet so capable downhill.
- **Tecnica Mach1 130 LV** -- Last year the R9.8 130 won Best Of for 130-flex alpine boot; the Mach1 is the subtly re-molded R9.8 with freeride touches instead of full plastic sole/race features. Proves "low volume" and "comfortable" aren't oxymorons; easy to work on.

### Best Of -- Outerwear [p. 165]
